## Summary

Frozen, Walt Disney Animation Studios' 53rd animated feature film, held its world premiere at Hollywood's El Capitan Theatre on November 19, 2013, and — after a brief limited release beginning November 22 — opened nationwide across the United States on November 27, 2013, the day before Thanksgiving. Directed by Chris Buck and Jennifer Lee and loosely inspired by Hans Christian Andersen's 1844 fairy tale "The Snow Queen," the film departed significantly from its source material to tell an original story of sisterhood, self-acceptance, and love.

The story centers on Elsa (voiced by Idina Menzel), a princess born with the magical ability to create and control ice and snow, and her younger sister Anna (Kristen Bell). When Elsa accidentally reveals her powers at her own coronation and flees the kingdom of Arendelle into self-imposed exile on a remote mountain, Anna sets out to find her — accompanied by an ice harvester named Kristoff (Jonathan Groff), his reindeer Sven, and a magically animated snowman named Olaf (Josh Gad). The film subverted traditional Disney princess conventions by centering its resolution on an act of sisterly love rather than romantic love.

## Production History

The development of Frozen at Walt Disney Animation Studios spanned several years and multiple creative reinventions. The studio had attempted adaptations of "The Snow Queen" dating back to the Walt Disney era of the 1940s, with the project proving notoriously difficult to adapt due to the complexity and episodic structure of Andersen's original tale. The breakthrough came when director Chris Buck and producer Peter Del Vecho brought in Jennifer Lee — who became the first woman to direct a Walt Disney Animation Studios feature film — and reconceived the story around the relationship between the two sisters.

The animation presented significant technical challenges, as the film required rendering convincing snow and ice with a level of visual realism and beauty not previously achieved in computer animation. Disney's effects team developed a specialized simulation system called "Matterhorn" to handle snow dynamics, and created approximately 2,000 individually designed snowflake patterns woven into Elsa's iconic ice dress. The total production budget was approximately $150 million.

Songs and score were composed by husband-and-wife team Kristen Anderson-Lopez and Robert Lopez. Lopez had previously won Tony Awards for the Broadway musicals *Avenue Q* and *The Book of Mormon*. The duo wrote eight original songs for the film, including "Let It Go," which became one of the best-selling singles of 2013–2014.

## Box Office and Awards

Frozen grossed $67.4 million in its opening weekend in the United States — a record for a Walt Disney Animation Studios film at the time — and went on to earn roughly $1.28 billion worldwide during its theatrical run, becoming the highest-grossing animated film ever made at that point — surpassing the previous record held by *Toy Story 3* ($1.067 billion) — and, by the end of its run, the fifth highest-grossing film of all time.

At the 86th Academy Awards in March 2014, Frozen won two Academy Awards:
- **Best Animated Feature**, defeating *The Croods*, *Despicable Me 2*, *Ernest & Celestine*, and *The Wind Rises*
- **Best Original Song** for "Let It Go," performed by Idina Menzel

"Let It Go" also won a Grammy Award for Best Song Written for Visual Media in 2015. Idina Menzel's performance of the song at the 86th Academy Awards ceremony — introduced by a famously mangled mispronunciation of her name by presenter John Travolta — itself became a cultural moment widely discussed in media.

## Cultural Impact

"Let It Go" became one of the defining pop-cultural artifacts of 2013–2014. The song charted internationally, was recorded in the 41 languages into which the film was translated and dubbed for international release, and generated hundreds of millions of YouTube views. Its themes of self-acceptance and releasing shame resonated broadly and were widely interpreted through multiple lenses, including LGBTQ+ readings of Elsa's story of hiding a fundamental aspect of herself before embracing it publicly.

The film's portrayal of female characters — particularly Elsa's arc of self-discovery and Anna's narrative agency independent of romantic love — contributed to ongoing conversations about gender representation in children's and family entertainment. The traditional Disney formula of a princess saved by a prince's romantic love was explicitly subverted and commented upon within the film's own narrative.

Frozen merchandise generated an estimated $531 million in retail sales in the United States alone within its first year, becoming one of the most successful entertainment merchandise campaigns in Disney's history. Theme park attractions, a Broadway musical adaptation (which opened in 2018), and a theatrical sequel, *Frozen II* (2019), extended the franchise's cultural footprint well beyond the initial 2013 release.
